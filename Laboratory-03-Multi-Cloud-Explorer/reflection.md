# Mission 3 Reflection

Completing this Multi-Cloud Explorer mission gave me a much deeper appreciation for how different the "big three" cloud providers really are, despite offering many overlapping services on the surface.

**Which cloud platform impressed you the most?**

Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most, mainly because of how tightly its AI and data analytics tools work together. Learning that Google created Kubernetes and still runs the most refined managed version of it (GKE) made me see GCP less as a "third option" and more as a specialist platform with real technical leadership in specific areas, even if it doesn't have AWS's overall service breadth. The idea that GCP was built on the same infrastructure powering Google Search and YouTube also gave me a new appreciation for the scale these companies operate at behind the scenes.

**What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?**

All three platforms are built around the same core building blocks: virtual machines, object storage, managed databases, identity and access management, and serverless compute. They all follow a pay-as-you-go pricing model, offer global infrastructure through regions and availability zones, and provide both a web console and command-line tools for managing resources. Despite different naming conventions (EC2 vs. Virtual Machines vs. Compute Engine), the underlying concepts of cloud computing — elasticity, on-demand provisioning, and managed services — are consistent across all three.

**How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?**

This was the biggest lesson from the client recommendation exercise. There's no single "best" cloud provider — the right choice depends entirely on the client's existing technology stack, budget constraints, technical needs, and growth expectations. A university already using Microsoft tools has a very different ideal platform than an AI research company needing specialized hardware for machine learning, even though both are just "businesses that need cloud services." This taught me that being a good cloud consultant means asking the right questions about a client's situation before recommending a technology, rather than defaulting to whichever platform is most popular or familiar.

**What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?**

I learned that recommending cloud platforms is less about knowing every technical feature and more about matching capabilities to real business problems. Building the comparison tables and decision matrix forced me to think about trade-offs instead of just listing what each platform can do. I also learned that the "right" answer can be justified in multiple ways depending on which factor (cost, integration, performance, scalability) is prioritized, which is closer to how real technical decisions get made in the industry.

**How has your GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio improved after completing this mission?**

My portfolio now includes structured, well-organized documentation comparing three major cloud providers, backed by research, comparison tables, and evidence-based recommendations rather than just definitions. Completing the Linux investigation and connecting it to cloud hosting options also shows a practical understanding of how on-premises systems map to cloud infrastructure. Overall, this lab added a more analytical and consultative layer to my portfolio, showing not just what I know about cloud platforms, but how I can apply that knowledge to solve real business problems.
